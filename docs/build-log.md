**Stages 0–5 complete. ✅ Stage 6, 6.5, 7 and 8 are COMPLETE. Stage 8 closed 2026-08-10 (doc v1.54) with phase 3. Stage 9 (launch) is next.**

📐 **/about's history row was made to start level, 2026-08-15 (doc v1.63).** Reported with a screenshot: the portrait on the left began well below the first text card on the right. One file, no migration.
  - 📌 **The cause is that a heading inside a grid column offsets that column and nothing else.** "History of MISA" was the first child of the left column, so it pushed the portrait down by its own height plus its margin while the right column started at the row's top — about 66px of misalignment. Moving the heading to a sibling *above* the grid is visually identical (it is left-aligned, and the portrait still sits directly beneath it) and both columns now begin on the same line. **Measured after: image top 982, first card top 982, both columns ending at 1490.**
  - ⚠️ **A hand-tuned `lg:mt-[66px]` on the right column would have been the wrong fix** even though it produces the same screenshot: the heading changes size at the `sm` breakpoint and wraps on narrow viewports, so the number is only correct at one width. Structure that cannot drift beats a measurement that can.
  - **`flex-1` came off the portrait in the same move.** It existed to make the image fill the old flex column; as a direct grid item under `items-stretch` that is already the behaviour, and a stale `flex-1` on a grid child is the kind of no-op that reads as load-bearing later.

⬜ **The gallery band's navy ground came off 2026-08-15 (doc v1.62).** Requested directly. One component file; no schema, no route, and **no change to the marquee geometry**.
  - 📌 **The lesson is that a background is a four-part edit.** `Hatch`'s "light tone on white grounds, navy tone on navy ones, never mixed" rule is what turns one visible change into four: dropping `on-navy bg-misa-blue` from the section forces the tiles from the navy hatch to the light one, their `border-white/28` frames to `misa-border` hairlines, and the "See all photos" eyebrow from white to `misa-blue`. `.on-navy` goes too — its only job is flipping the focus ring to white, which is exactly wrong on white. Miss any one and the band is half-dressed in a way that reads as a rendering bug rather than a design choice. The component header now lists all four, so putting the navy back is one reversible decision.
  - **The v1.60 marquee measurements are unaffected.** The wrap defect was `(copies − 1) × groupWidth` against viewport width; nothing in it depended on the colour behind the tiles. What did need fixing was the *prose* — this log, the invariants and the component header all said "bare navy" where they meant "bare ground", which would have read as stale the moment somebody checked.
  - ⚠️ **The home page now runs white from the hero chevron to the projects band** — mission, gallery, Activities in sequence. That is what was asked for, and the band still reads as its own section on the strength of the tile rows and the eyebrow link, but the handoff used this navy as the page's mid-point contrast. Recorded as a deliberate divergence from the prototype, not an oversight.
  - 🪤 **The padding had to follow the background, and this was caught by the officer rather than by us.** The band carried `py-12 sm:pb-14` — that was the navy field's *inset*, and it only read as padding because there was a field to inset from. With the field gone it stacked on the neighbouring sections' padding instead: **112px of dead air above the tiles and 108px below**, on a page whose other seams run 48–64px. Now `pb-2 sm:pb-3`, with the rhythm coming from the mission's `sm:py-16` above and Activities' `pt-13` below, the way every other seam on this page works. **Measured after: 81px above the tiles, 82px below, 46px from the mission paragraph down to the eyebrow link.** ⚠️ Restoring the navy means restoring the inset with it — a full-bleed field with no padding crops tight to the tiles.
  - **The generalisable point: removing a background is not a one-property edit.** Two of the four colour changes and the padding all existed *because of* the field, and none of them names it. The component header now lists the whole set.
  - ✅ **Verified in a browser against the production build**, not just by diff: the section's computed `background-color` is `rgba(0,0,0,0)`, the tiles carry `hatch-light` + `border-misa-border`, `.on-navy` is down to the two elements that are still navy (the hero and the projects band), and the spacing numbers above are measured off the rendered page.

🎠 **The home page marquee was never seamless, and was fixed 2026-08-14 (doc v1.60).** Reported as "it stops or moves left to right interchangeably, and has a noticeable end". **No migration, two files.**
  - 🪤 **The cause is a recipe that is only conditionally correct.** "Duplicate the tile group exactly twice and translate `-50%`" is the standard CSS marquee, and it is seamless **only when one group is wider than the viewport** — a precondition nothing in the code stated and nobody checked. Neither track met it. Measured on production at a 1646px viewport: the top track's group is **1360px** (5 x 260 + 5 x 12) and the bottom's **1272px** (6 x 200 + 6 x 12), so at the end of a cycle each track had translated a full group width and its right edge sat **286px** and **374px** short of the viewport — bare navy, then a snap back.
  - 📌 **Two of the three reported symptoms were the same defect wearing different hats.** The lower track runs in reverse, so its hole opens at the *start* of its cycle while the upper track's opens at the *end*; the rows therefore broke at opposite moments and read as though they were interchanging direction. The opposed directions are the handoff's spec and were correct — that was confirmed with the officer before touching them, precisely because the report could have been read as a complaint about the design.
  - **The third symptom was unrelated and simpler:** `.marquee-left:hover, .marquee-right:hover { animation-play-state: paused }`. The tracks span the full width, so a mouse resting anywhere over the band froze that row. The handoff only said to *consider* pausing on hover; it is gone. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` still stops both, which is an accessibility requirement rather than a nicety.
  - **The fix derives the geometry instead of assuming it.** `GAP` and `TILE` are constants in the component, and `groupWidth`, the copy count (`ceil(MAX_VIEWPORT / groupWidth) + 1` → 4 and 5) and the translate distance all fall out of them. 🔓 **The distance is an exact pixel value passed to CSS as `--marquee-shift`, never a percentage** — a percentage is implicitly a function of the copy count, and that coupling is exactly what let the original break without any error. One `marquee-scroll` keyframe block now serves both tracks, the lower one via `animation-direction: reverse`, replacing the near-duplicate `mq`/`mqr` pair.
  - ⚠️ **`MAX_VIEWPORT` (4000px) is a real ceiling, not a margin** — past it there are not enough copies and the gap comes back. Documented at its definition with the formula, the way `MEMBER_SCAN_LIMIT` and `RATE_LIMIT_MAX` are.
  - 🪤 **The lesson worth keeping is about verification, not CSS.** The original shipped after being *watched* and looking fine — a 38-second loop hides a defect that is visible for about two seconds at the wrap. The check that finds it is numeric and takes one call: pause the animation at points across a full cycle (negative `animation-delay` + `animationPlayState: paused`) and assert the track's right edge is never left of `window.innerWidth`. Run over 40 steps after the fix, both tracks pass with 2468px and 3442px of surplus.
  - ⚠️ **Real-time motion could not be confirmed in the automation browser** — the controlled tab suspends frames, so `requestAnimationFrame` never fires and `animation.currentTime` does not advance there (the same reason zoomed screenshots time out on this page). The geometry is verified numerically across the whole cycle and the keyframe interpolation was verified by sampling it; **that the band animates at all in a real browser is evidenced by the bug report itself**, not by a measurement taken here.

🎨 **The public UI overhaul shipped 2026-08-14 (doc v1.58).** **No migration, no Server Action, no RLS change** — presentation and content constants only. A design handoff arrived as `docs/Texas MISA website UI mockups/design_handoff_misa_website/`: five high-fidelity `.dc.html` prototypes (Homepage, About, Projects, Gallery, Officers) plus a README carrying the full token, typography and spacing spec, and an `assets/` folder with 11 event photos and four partner logos. The Stage 2 recreation of the Squarespace site is superseded — periwinkle `#7286ab` and Poppins/Roboto Slab become navy `#16305c` and Barlow + Barlow Condensed, rounded pills become square corners, and the partner wordmarks become real logos.
  - 📌 **The token NAMES did not change, and that was the point.** `--misa-blue` was repointed rather than renamed, so ~410 `misa-blue` / `misa-panel` / `font-display` usages across the admin area kept working and the admin inherited the identity without a screen-by-screen rewrite. Three places set dark text on `bg-misa-blue` and had to flip to white (the site header, `admin-nav.tsx`, `/admin/login`); `wordmark.tsx` now draws in `currentColor` so it renders navy on white and white on navy from one definition.
  - 🔓 **Officer sign-in moved from the footer to the nav, reversing a documented invariant.** The footer link existed because the header had no room: eight links plus four social icons around an absolutely centred wordmark, measured at 1646px overflowing by 199px. The redesign spends the room the other way — socials move to the footer, Contact leaves the nav — so the left group is five short uppercase items. **Re-measured after the change: 285px clearance left of the wordmark and 312px right at 1280, and the left group ends at x=331 against a wordmark starting at x=791 at 1646.** The 🪤 stands: the wordmark wins the z-order, so an overflowing item still disappears silently rather than wrapping.
  - ⚠️ **`/contact` is routed but unlinked.** The handoff has no Contact page and no Contact nav item. The route, its email list and its still-disconnected form all survive and got the new skin; the mobile sheet still carries it, because that panel stacks and has no wordmark to clear. §5 keeps its row with the caveat spelled out.
  - ⚠️ **Officer headshots are placeholders on purpose, and the handoff's own README is why.** It ships headshots and says the photo-to-name pairing "was never supplied". A real student's face under another real student's name is a worse failure than a framed hatched box, so `Officer.photo` is optional and unset; filling one in switches that card. The mono "pairing to be confirmed" note the prototype carries is unnecessary as a result.
  - 🪤 **Unlayered global CSS beats every Tailwind v4 utility, and it presented as an invisible button.** `@import "tailwindcss"` emits utilities into `@layer utilities`; a rule written outside any layer wins regardless of specificity. A bare `a { color: var(--misa-blue) }` therefore overrode `text-white` on every link, and the header's Check In button rendered navy text on a navy fill — a solid rectangle with nothing in it. Found by reading `getComputedStyle` off the element, not by staring at the screenshot. Element defaults now live in `@layer base` and decorative classes in `@layer components`.
  - 🪤 **The scroll reveal has two halves for a build reason.** The handoff says not to port the prototypes' throttled scroll handler, so it is an `IntersectionObserver` — but `revealDelay()` exported from the same `"use client"` module could not be *called* on the server, and `/about` failed to prerender with "Attempted to call revealDelay() from the server". The helper is now `reveal.tsx` (server-safe, must never gain the directive) and the observer is `reveal-observer.tsx`, mounted once in the public layout. That split is also what keeps every animated section a Server Component: a page marks a `<div data-reveal="up">` and stays server-rendered.
  - 🔓 **The hidden start state is scoped to `html.js`, set by an inline `<head>` script.** Unscoped, a visitor with JavaScript disabled gets a blank page — which is what the prototypes' 250ms rAF-availability probe was working around. Done in an effect instead, the content paints and then blanks. The script runs during HTML parsing, before first paint (Next's own "preventing flash before hydration" pattern). `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` cancels both the reveal and the marquees; the prototypes honour neither and the handoff says they should.
  - 🪤 **`PhotoFrame` renders with next/image's `fill`, and the About history portrait is why.** With an intrinsically sized image the frame grows to the photo's natural height and leaves a large void beside the column next to it — the handoff calls this out explicitly. `fill` absolutely positions the image so the frame's own `flex:1; min-height` decides. `PhotoBlock` is the deliberate opposite for the gallery masonry, where varied intrinsic heights are the point.
  - **Content moved into `lib/site.ts` rather than into the pages.** `PILLARS` became `ACTIVITIES`; `FAQ` and the history paragraphs left `about/page.tsx`; `PROJECTS`, `PROJECT_STATS`, `PROJECTS_INTRO`, `PARTNERS` (with logo paths), the `PHOTOS` manifest and the `GALLERY_*` display lists are new. Nothing was dropped: the seventh FAQ answer, about student projects, is verbatim the Projects page's intro paragraph, which is where the handoff moved it. `photo(id)` throws on an unknown id rather than returning undefined.
  - **The gallery's chips and Load more are real.** They are presentational in the prototype and the handoff says they need behaviour, so `gallery-grid.tsx` — the one client component among the five designed pages — filters the static manifest and pages it. The count beside the chips is derived from what is on screen, so it cannot drift the way the prototype's hardcoded "24 photos" already had. ⚠️ `PHOTOS[].category` is a **provisional guess**: the shoot dates are known but the events were never labelled.
  - **What is deliberately still a placeholder:** every activity and project image (hatched boxes with mono captions, exactly as the handoff intends), officer headshots, and the real MISA logo — the README names `misa.zip - 1.png` but it is **not in the bundle**, so the CSS wordmark stays.
  - ✅ **Verified:** `npm run lint` and `npm run build` clean, `tests/docs.test.ts` green, and a browser pass over all ten public routes against the remote (all 200). The mobile breakpoints are reasoned rather than measured — the prototypes are desktop-only fixed layouts with no breakpoints authored, and the browser tool in use could not resize the window.

🔓 **Officer invite links shipped 2026-08-10 (doc v1.57) — migrations 24 and 25, and the §6 claim they invalidate.** Officers are added by an expiring, single-use link from `/admin/officers` instead of a `scripts/create-officer.mjs` run that needs the production service role key in someone's environment. §2.3 says officers turn over every year, so the old path made routine turnover either one technical person's job or a reason to spread the service key around.
  - 🔓 **This deliberately opens the privilege-escalation path §6's risk register said did not exist**, so that row is **rewritten** rather than left quietly stale, and §9 gains **#13**. A valid unexpired unclaimed invite is a *capability*. What contains it is in the Invariants below.
  - ⚠️ **Migration 25 then WEAKENED it on request: the invited address is now optional.** Blank means an **open invite** — a bearer credential redeemable by whoever holds the link, into any mailbox they control, with no record of who it was meant for. §6 and §9 #13 are both amended rather than left asserting the stronger rule. It exists because pinned-only failed in a real way: an officer often does not know which mailbox somebody reads, and a wrong guess produced a link that simply did not work. **The role stays pinned either way** — an open invite lets the holder choose who they are, never what they may do, so the worst case is an unintended *officer* account that is auditable and revocable.
  - 🔓 **Only the token's SHA-256 is stored.** The row is not a credential, which is what makes an invite list safe to render. A test asserts the token appears nowhere in the entire row, not merely that `token_hash` differs.
  - ⚠️ **Claim before create.** The conditional `UPDATE` is both the single-use and the concurrency guard; a failure after it burns the invite and grants nothing, which is the recoverable direction. **Four tests were confirmed to go red by dropping the predicate** — a guard never seen red is a guard nobody knows works.
  - 🔓 **The role comes off the stored row, always** — `inviteAcceptSchema` has no `role` key at all, so a tampered POST has nothing to reach; structural, not a check. The **email** does too *whenever the invite pinned one*, and the open path uses a **separate** schema so the pinned branch still has no parsed address in scope.
  - **Any officer may invite (§9 #6's reasoning applied to privilege for the first time); the one refusal is self-revocation**, which is what keeps team-wide lockout unreachable from the UI.
  - **Nothing is emailed** — no SMTP exists (the built-in sender is 2/hour and not for production), which is also what makes Supabase's own `inviteUserByEmail` unusable. The officer copies the link and sends it themselves, adding no service to hand over at turnover.
  - ✅ **Migration 24 is applied to the remote and deployed** (pushed 2026-08-10, verified by `migration list --linked` and by reading the schema back; production serves `/officer-invite/[token]` with `noindex, nofollow, nocache`).
  - ✅ **Migration 25 (`open_invites`) is applied to local and the remote, and deployed** (2026-08-10, verified by reading the remote schema back — `email` nullable, RLS on, 0 rows — and by GitHub's deployment API reporting `success`). 🪤 A chunk-hash poll reported the earlier deploy as not landed when it had; the alphabetically-first chunk does not change when its content does not. **Use `gh api repos/.../deployments` to confirm a deploy, not a fingerprint guessed off the HTML.**
  - 📌 **The redemption page carries no help text and no client-side length rule** (requested 2026-08-10) — heading, labels, fields. Validation is therefore entirely server-side. The password floor moved 12 → **6**, mirroring GoTrue's own minimum rather than stating a policy: `createUser` runs *after* the claim, so a shorter password would burn a single-use link and grant nothing. ⚠️ `scripts/create-officer.mjs` still enforces 12; the asymmetry is deliberate.
  - 🪤 **`officer_invites` had to join `seed.sql`'s wipe list, and `db reset` cannot show you that.** Reset drops the database rather than running the deletes — which is exactly how the `shirt_size` drift hid. Two failure modes, both real: `created_by` references `auth.users` with no cascade, so an invite from the seed officer **aborts the whole re-seed** with `officer_invites_created_by_fkey` (confirmed by removing the delete and re-running); and more quietly, an invite is a **live credential**, so a wipe that misses it clears every member and leaves a working link behind. Asserted at 0 in the seed's check block.

✅ **Stage 8 phase 3 shipped 2026-08-10** — **error boundaries, and the empty-vs-error correction.** **No migration.**
  - 🔓 **The substantive half was never the files.** The house pattern for a failed read was `x.error ? [] : x.data`, which renders the EMPTY state — so a broken query did not say "something went wrong", it made an affirmative false claim. The five admin list pages already got this right; the rot was in the detail pages and `lib/`.
  - 🔓 **`fetchOfficerNames` returning an empty Map was the widest one, and a comment called it "cosmetic".** `describeOfficer` reads absence from the map as "no `admin_profiles` row", i.e. a REVOKED officer — so one failed read relabelled **every officer on every screen** as "a former officer": the ledger, both dues surfaces, the member detail, the audit trail. Exactly the regression that function's own header records as fixed, reintroduced through the error path, on the log whose purpose is saying who did what. It returns a discriminated `OfficerNames` now, and `describeOfficer` has a **fourth** case.
  - 🐛 **`lib/lookup.ts` had FIVE swallows, not four** — the fifth being the term read, which skipped the events query entirely. On a member's own page: every event painted missed, the "Waiting on an officer" section silently gone, and "You're an official member for Fall 2026" printable directly above "No payment of yours covers a term yet". **Fails the whole lookup now** — the five numbers interlock, so a partial view invites the wrong conclusion.
  - 🐛 **FOUR `notFound()`-on-failure conflations**, not two: `members/[id]`, `events/[id]`, `attendance/[id]`, `dues/[id]`. Each logged the error, discarded it, and told an officer the record does not exist. They **throw** now — a read that broke belongs in the error boundary, not behind a 404.
  - 🪤 **`events/page.tsx` failing `current_term` SILENTLY WIDENED the view** from "this term" to every event ever, with the control reading "all" so nothing looked wrong. There is no honest fallback — without the term the page cannot know what it should be showing — so it throws.
  - 🪤 **`error.tsx` never wraps the `layout.tsx` in its own segment**, so `app/admin/error.tsx` exists specifically to catch `AdminShellLayout`'s `requireOfficer()`. **Both boundaries were exercised by making the layout throw**, and they are genuinely different screens — the parent one renders without the admin nav, because the nav is what failed.
  - 🪤 **`loading.tsx` and per-widget `<Suspense>` conflict**, so there is exactly one `loading.tsx` and no Suspense: the prerenderer stops at the first boundary walking up, and a high `loading.js` masks granular streaming behind a full-page skeleton. It also converts a `notFound()` 404 into a 200 — accepted, every admin route is noindex.
  - 📌 **`unstable_retry`, not `reset`** (Next 16.2): `reset()` cannot recover a Server Component error, which is every error these boundaries will see. Boundaries render `error.digest`, because production replaces the message with a generic one.
  - **`_components/notice.tsx` gives error its own tone.** Not cosmetic: "no data" and "read failed" rendered in the identical blue until now, so fixing the logic without it would have left the two indistinguishable on screen.

✅ **Stage 8 phase 2 shipped 2026-08-10** — **the attendance and adjustment archives.** **Migration 23** (one new `admin_audit` entity type).
  - 🔓 **The extraction came first, and it is the point of the phase.** The roster export is safe because `applyMemberFilter` is a *shared* translation — the route re-runs provably the query the screen counted. Points and attendance had no equivalent: their predicates were inline in the page bodies, each with **its own copy of the Central half-open date bound**, and an export route would have been the third copy. `lib/ledger-filters.ts` is now the one translation both the screens and the routes use.
  - 📌 **Measured proof the shared date bound is right**: `?to=2026-08-04` returns **170** submissions. A UTC-anchored bound would return **168** — the seed has 2 rows submitted on the evening of Aug 4 Central, which is exactly the 5-hour slice `.lte()` silently drops.
  - 🐛 **Extracting it fixed a live 500.** Neither page validated `from`/`to`, and `centralWallTimeToInstant("yesterday", …)` does not return an Invalid Date — it **throws** at `lib/events.ts:99`. A hand-typed `?from=yesterday` was a 500 on both ledgers. Also tightened: `?status=xyz` on the queue used to reach `.eq()` and match nothing, which is "a filter with no control on screen" narrowing invisibly.
  - 🪤 **`.order("id")` is a TIE-BREAK on a chunked read, and I missed it on the first pass.** The exports page in chunks of 1000; rows tied on `submitted_at`/`awarded_at` have no defined relative order, so a tie straddling a `.range()` boundary duplicates one row and **drops another** — surfacing as missing data in an archive. Not theoretical: the seed alone has **4 ties in each table**, and a room checking in together or one multi-member grant produces them in bulk.
  - 🪤 **`signedPoints` must never reach a spreadsheet.** It renders **U+2212 MINUS**, not ASCII hyphen — so `guardFormula` correctly ignores it *and Excel refuses to parse it as a number*, leaving the column inert text that will not sum. Points go out as `{ kind: "number" }`.
  - 🐛 **The first real export showed a defect no test would have caught**: the default field set omitted `voided_date`, and the ledger's `state` filter defaults to `all` — so a voided +8 grant came out **byte-identical to a live one**. The screen distinguishes them with a strikethrough a file cannot carry. `voided_date` is in the defaults now.
  - ⚠️ **`parseFieldSelection` gained a `defaults` parameter.** It falls back by *filtering the catalogue*, so passing the member defaults to a ledger catalogue — which shares no key with it — would resolve to zero fields and emit a file with **no columns at all**. A test asserts every default key exists in its catalogue.
  - **The archives carry more than the screens show**: attendance gains `resolved_at`/`resolved_by`/`resolution_note`, adjustments gain the void columns. That is what "archival" means.

  - **`start_term` is picked from a derived list, never typed** — `startTermOptions` steps off `termOf(paid_at)` by term index, and the action re-checks membership of that list rather than trusting the POST. It appends a stored value outside the window, because a `<select>` with no matching `<option>` renders blank and the next save would rewrite a real answer.
  - **The import is two steps over one CSV**, and the text lives **only in the officer's browser** between them — no staging table, no temp file. `commitImport` **re-parses** rather than accepting the preview's output; both actions share one `planImport`, so the commit runs the identical code path and cannot diverge from what was shown. Same posture as `/attend`'s `step=confirm`.
  - 🔓 **`upsert` with `ignoreDuplicates` is the write**, and it is the first use of upsert in application code. It makes "re-importing is a no-op" a **database** guarantee rather than an app-level pre-check a concurrent import could race past, and the `.select()` then returns only the rows actually inserted — so **requested − returned is the duplicate count**, with no second query to disagree with it.
  - ⚠️ **Next caps a Server Action request at 1MB by default.** `MAX_IMPORT_BYTES` (512 KB) and `MAX_IMPORT_ROWS` (2000) sit below it so the officer gets a sentence naming the limit rather than an opaque framework error. Both **refuse; neither truncates**.
  - 🐛 **`start_term` must be set by the application, not left to the column default** — found in the phase-2 walkthrough, where the preview said a June payment counted as Spring and the stored row said Fall. The default is `term_of(now())`, the *import* time, and **a Postgres column default cannot reference another column**, so it can never ask `term_of(paid_at)` the way its own comment claims. Those differ for every statement uploaded after a term boundary, which is the ordinary case. `termOf()` in `lib/dues.ts` is the mirror, so the term stays derived rather than typed (§4.7).
  - ⚠️ **`lib/roster-index.ts` is uncapped and deliberately not `fetchMemberOptions`** — that caps at `MEMBER_SCAN_LIMIT`, filters to active members, and never selects `normalized_eid`. Matching against a truncated roster reports `unmatched` for someone who *is* on the roster: silent truncation landing on money. 🪤 But uncapped ≠ one unbounded request — it pages in 1000s, because the hosted project applies its own `max_rows`.
    - 📌 **It was `lib/dues-roster.ts` until phase 7b**, when the roster import became a second caller and it gained `emailLower`. This bullet still named the old file in the present tense a phase later — found in the phase-9 read-through, and a good illustration of what that pass is for: the *rule* was right the whole time and the file it pointed at had not existed for a day.
  - **Migration 19** carries `dues_payments`, the `app_settings` price columns (3000 / 5000 cents), `term_index` / `term_at_index` / `next_term` / `terms_from`, `'dues_payment'` in the audit entity types, `dues_paid_current_term` appended to `member_directory`, and the three reserved dues keys. Applied from scratch via `db reset`, so it rebuilds from the repo alone.
  - 🪤 **The real Venmo export format was recorded from an actual statement, and every part of it was a surprise**: the header is on **line 3** with a leading empty column, the amount is `- $18.50` with the sign as a separate token before the `$`, the footer is a **quoted field spanning multiple lines** (so a `split("\n")` parser breaks on the last record of every file), and non-transaction rows are identified by an **empty `ID`** rather than by position. Full table in the spec doc.
  - ⚠️ **A Venmo timestamp carries no timezone** (`2026-09-03T19:22:00`), so `new Date(raw)` reads it as UTC and lands a 9pm Central payment five hours early — the `new Date("2026-09-01T18:00")` trap in a new place. Decided: **Central wall time**, attached via `centralWallTimeToInstant`.
  - 📌 **Terms are ordered by an integer index**, not by iterating `next_term` and never by string compare: `Spring 2026 → 4052`, `Fall 2026 → 4053`. Immutable with no recursion (a generated column requires that) and it gives the schema a correct total order, which is what the lexicographic trap actually needs.
